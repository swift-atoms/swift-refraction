# swift-refraction

Use this package when light crosses from one medium into another and you need the physics of it: which way it bends, how much of it the boundary sends back, how a medium's index varies with the wavelength, and what a lens or a ball of such a medium does to what is seen through it. Start from `Refraction.Index` for a medium (vacuum is `Refraction.Index.vacuum`, 1), `Refraction.Interface(from:into:)` for the boundary, `Refraction.Cauchy` for a medium catalogued by its index at the d line and its Abbe number, and `Refraction.Lens` and `Refraction.Ball` for lenses. Every type is generic over a binary floating-point scalar; the angle form of Snell's law also needs the scalar's circular functions, which swift-numeric gives `Double`, `Float` and `Float16`. It leaves out what is not refraction's: turning wavelengths into a colour (colorimetry), polarisation states beyond averaging the two, absorption, and tracing rays through a scene, which is the renderer's.

## Laws

- An index is a positive number, the speed of light in vacuum over its speed in the medium; nothing checks it. `Refraction.Wavelength` is the wavelength in vacuum, held in nanometres and convertible to metres, with the Fraunhofer lines `d` (587.5618 nm), `F` (486.1327 nm) and `C` (656.2725 nm).
- `Interface.refracted(_:at:)` is Snell's law in vector form for a unit direction and the boundary's unit normal on the side the light comes from: the result is a unit vector in their plane whose sine to the normal is `ratio` times the incoming one, where `ratio` is the incident over the transmitted index. `refracted(incidence:)` is the same law for the angle to the normal. Equal indices bend nothing and light meeting the boundary head-on passes straight, both up to rounding.
- Where `ratio · sin θ` exceeds 1, on the way out of the denser medium beyond `critical`, the outcome is `.totalInternalReflection`, carrying the mirrored direction (`Refraction.reflected(_:at:)`, the law of reflection) or the angle of incidence. `critical` is nil where light goes into an index at least its own.
- `reflectance(cosine:)` is the share of unpolarised light the boundary reflects, the mean of Fresnel's equations for the two polarisations: `((n₁ − n₂) / (n₁ + n₂))²` head-on, rising to 1 at a grazing angle, 1 beyond the critical angle, and 0 at every angle between equal indices. `transmittance(cosine:)` comes from the transmission coefficients and the ratio of power flows, and the two add up to 1 up to rounding. The cosine is held to 0 through 1.
- `Refraction.Schlick` is Schlick's approximation, a separate and cheaper thing: exact head-on, within four hundredths of Fresnel's equations between vacuum and an index of 1.5 either way, poorest near a grazing angle between close indices. From the denser side it takes the cosine of the transmitted angle, and beyond the critical angle it is 1.
- `Cauchy.index(at:)` is Cauchy's law `A + B / λ²` with `B` chosen so that `(n_d − 1) / (n_F − n_C)` is the Abbe number and `A` so that the index at the d line is the given index exactly. Shorter wavelengths have the higher index. An infinite Abbe number, the default, gives one index for every wavelength, and so does one that is not positive or a wavelength that is not positive. The law fits transparent media in the visible range, not near an absorption band, and it may give an index below 1 far from it.
- `Lens.power` is the thick-lens lensmaker's equation with the index relative to the surroundings; a radius is positive where its centre lies beyond the surface along the light, and an infinite radius is flat. `Ball.focalLength` is `n r / (2 (n − 1))` from the centre, equal to its lens's. `Ball.enlargement(at:)` is the lateral magnification of an object that far behind the ball's surface, seen from far in front: `n / (2 − n)` at contact, growing to infinity at the back focal point, negative (turned over) beyond.
